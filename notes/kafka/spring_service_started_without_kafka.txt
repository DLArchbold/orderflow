Your inventory-service is running, but it can't reach Kafka. It has been retrying about once a second and logging a warning each time.

## What each line means

The same three lines repeat every second:

- **`Node -1 disconnected`** (INFO): the Kafka client lost or failed to open its connection. Node `-1` isn't a real broker. It's the placeholder Kafka uses for the bootstrap address you configured, before it has learned the real broker list.
- **`Connection to node -1 (localhost/127.0.0.1:9092) could not be established. Node may not be available.`** (WARN): nothing answered on `localhost:9092`. The connection was refused or timed out.
- **`Bootstrap broker localhost:9092 (id: -1 rack: null) disconnected`** (WARN): the client has no working broker, so it can't join its consumer group.

The rest of the line shows who is complaining:
- `[ntainer#0-0-C-1]` is a Spring Kafka listener container thread.
- `clientId=consumer-inventory-service-1` and `groupId=inventory-service` are inventory-service's consumer.

## Why it's happening

Nothing is listening on port 9092 on your machine. The most likely causes:

1. **Kafka isn't running.** This is the usual one. Step 11a starts inventory-service alone, before the full compose from 11b. Postgres is up, but Kafka isn't.
2. **The container is running but not reachable from the host.** This happens when the compose file's advertised listener doesn't match what the host connects to. It's the pitfall I flagged in 11b.
3. **Docker Desktop isn't running** or the container exited. Check `docker ps`.

## Is it a problem?

- **For step 11a, no.** Its Done-when is health UP and the Flyway tables in the `inventory` database. The warnings don't stop the app from starting. The consumer just keeps retrying in the background, and when Kafka appears it will connect on its own.
- **It may make `/actuator/health` report DOWN**, if the Kafka health indicator is enabled. If it does, either start Kafka or temporarily disable the consumer.
- **It floods the log**, which makes real errors hard to see.

## What to do

1. Run `docker ps` and look for a Kafka container.
2. If there isn't one, start the full stack: `docker compose -f phase2/infra/docker-compose.full.yml up -d`.
3. If it is running, check the logs (`docker logs <kafka-container>`) and the advertised listeners setting. Host apps should connect via `localhost:9092`.
4. Once Kafka is up, the warnings should stop within a few seconds without restarting inventory-service.
